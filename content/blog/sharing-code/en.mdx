At Bamse I work on three e-commerce apps for Henry Schein brands: Dental Cremer, Dental Speed and Utilidades Clínicas. They share most of their business logic, but for years each lived in its own repository — three copies of every component, three PRs for every fix.

## The cost of three repos

Every feature was implemented once and ported twice. Versions drifted, dependency upgrades multiplied, and setup for a new developer took days. The apps looked like siblings but were maintained like strangers.

## One monorepo, three apps

We migrated to a Turborepo monorepo. Each app keeps its own entry point and brand configuration, while everything shareable moved into workspace packages:

```text
apps/
  dental-cremer/
  dental-speed/
  utilidades-clinicas/
packages/
  ui/          # design system (Styled Components)
  core/        # business rules, API clients
  config/      # eslint, tsconfig, jest presets
```

## Private packages as contracts

The shared packages are published privately to NPM with semantic versioning. That forces us to treat internal code like a public API: documented props, changelogs, and no reaching into another package’s internals.

## The design system holds it together

A theme-driven design system built with Styled Components lets each brand ship its own colors and typography while reusing the exact same components. Visual consistency stopped being a code-review chore and became a property of the architecture.

## Results

Development time dropped roughly 30%, code reuse went way up, and the three apps hold a 4.9/5 average rating across the App Store and Google Play. The biggest win, though, is trust: a fix in one app is a fix in all three.
